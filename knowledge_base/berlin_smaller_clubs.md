# Smaller Berlin clubs, collectives and what to look for

## OXI

- Location: Friedrichshain, on Wiesenweg, in a former club complex.
- Space: large garden, multiple indoor floors, chill out zones and dark rooms, all with high end Lambda Labs sound.
- Vibe: queer and FLINTA safer space, explicitly body, sex and age positive, with a strong no photo, consent first culture.
- Music: house and techno in various shades, often with label showcases, day to night marathons and vinyl only floors.
- What to wear: expressive but practical outfits that you can dance in all day; avoid anything that reads as mocking queer or kink culture.

- Space: several industrial rooms and a big wooded garden.

## Salon zur Wilden Renate

- Location: Alt Stralau, near the Spree in Friedrichshain.
- Space: old apartment maze with three main floors, many smaller rooms and a courtyard.
- Vibe: surreal house party energy, with each room curated for a different mood.
- Music: from electro, breakbeat and acid (for example nights like Hi Res) to house and trippy techno.
- Parties: immersive nights like House of Lunacy for kink and costume, plus resident led house and techno series.
- What to wear: club casual on regular nights, full costume on themed ones. Always read the event text.

## Club der Visionaere

- Location: Am Flutgraben, on the canal between Kreuzberg and Treptow.
- Space: wooden decks over the water, small dancefloor, mostly open air.
- Vibe: intimate afterhours and warm up spot, minimal heads, lot of regulars.
- Music: minimal house and techno, often with unannounced sets from big names.
- What to wear: summer bar clothes, trainers or sandals that can handle wooden planks and spilled drinks.

## Other small and unique spaces

- Golden Gate, Heidegluhen, smaller Kreuzberg basements and rotating gallery or bar spaces all host collectives that may not even have proper websites.
- The best way to find them is via Resident Advisor listings, word of mouth, Telegram and Instagram of DJs and crews you like.
- Look for labels and crews, not only club logos. Many of the most interesting nights are one offs in borrowed rooms.

## How to read a flyer and prepare

- Always check the promoter or party name, not just the venue. That tells you a lot about expected crowd and dress.
- Read the event description for hints: words like queer, FLINTA, sex positive, kink, safer space, experimental or live hardware each imply different expectations.
- Check the start and end time. A 14:00 start in a garden reads very differently from a midnight warehouse.
- For kink or costume nights, start outfit planning early. Berlin rewards effort and creativity more than shiny brand names.
