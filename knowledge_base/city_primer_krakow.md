# City Primer: Krakow, Poland

## Scene character
Krakow is Poland's second city and the country's cultural capital. Its electronic music scene is smaller than Warsaw's but has a strong identity rooted in the city's art and student culture. The scene skews toward house, deep house, disco and leftfield electronic rather than the harder techno that dominates Warsaw's Smolna.

Krakow also has a growing techno contingent, particularly in the former factory and industrial spaces around Zablocie and the city's south.

## Key venues

### Forum Przestrzenie
- A large communist-era hotel (Hotel Forum, built 1988, abandoned for years) on the Vistula riverbank, converted into a cultural centre. The swimming pool has become a renowned open-air party space in summer. House, disco and techno events; striking architecture.
- The open-air pool parties are among the most visually distinctive club experiences in Central Europe.

### Drukarnia
- A club in a former printing house (drukarnia means printing house) in Zablocie, the former industrial quarter. Long-standing underground venue for techno and electronic music in Krakow.

### Hevre
- A venue in the former Jewish quarter (Kazimierz), in a converted synagogue. Cultural programming with club nights; eclectic sound, house and soul.

### Prozak 2.0
- A central Krakow nightclub, more accessible and mainstream, but historically significant as a long-running club in the city.

## Scene note
Krakow is worth including as a diversity anchor for Central European scenes distinct from Warsaw's harder industrial techno. The city rewards evening exploration: Kazimierz is one of Central Europe's most atmospheric neighbourhoods, and the contrast between Kazimierz's Jewish heritage and disco-in-the-synagogue culture is genuinely unique.

## Suits
- House, disco, and leftfield electronic fans who want Central European character and accessible prices. Forum Przestrzenie's pool parties are a singular experience for anyone visiting Poland in summer.
