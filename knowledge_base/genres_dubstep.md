# Dubstep

## What it is

Dubstep emerged in South London around 2000-2001, growing out of UK garage, dub reggae, and 2-step. The original sound, dark, minimal, bass-heavy, with a half-time feel, was created in nights like Forward>> at Plastic People in Brixton. It bore little resemblance to the aggressive "brostep" that became internationally famous a decade later.

## BPM and rhythmic signature

- **BPM range:** 138-142 BPM, but because of the half-time snare on beat 3, it *feels* like 69-71 BPM. This displaced, slow-feeling rhythm over a fast tempo is dubstep's defining structural feature.
- **Snare/clap:** On beat 3 (not 2 and 4 like most house/techno). This is the "half-time" feel, the most identifiable dubstep marker.
- **Bass:** The bass line is the melody in dubstep. Deep, dark, wobbling or sustained sub-bass. The spaces between notes are as important as the notes.

## How to recognise it

- The snare/clap on beat 3 is the clearest marker, count 1-2-3-4 and the hit comes on 3.
- Sub-bass that you feel more than hear. The low-end is the emotional centre.
- Lots of space and silence, gaps in the rhythm feel heavy.
- Original dubstep feels dark, empty, and noir. Not aggressive in the way of later commercial dubstep.

## Subgenres

- **Original dubstep:** Benga, Skream, Digital Mystikz, Loefah. Dark and minimal.
- **Post-dubstep:** James Blake, Mount Kimbie. More introspective and song-structured.
- **Brostep:** Skrillex, Flux Pavilion. Aggressive, maximalist, stadium-oriented. Largely a North American phenomenon.

## Key artists and labels

Digital Mystikz, Benga, Skream, Loefah, Burial. Labels: DMZ Records, Hyperdub (also post-dubstep and grime-adjacent).

## Berlin context

Dubstep and its descendants (UK bass, post-dubstep) appear in Berlin's more eclectic programme, //about blank, OHM, and Tresor's experimental nights. Hyperdub artists tour Berlin regularly. Not a dominant sound at the major techno clubs but present in the city's experimental underground.
