# Rave Wellness — Supplies, Recovery, and Harm Reduction

## What to Bring

A practical packing list for a long Berlin night:

**Essential**
- **Earplugs** — not optional for any set over 2 hours at a serious venue. Custom-moulded plugs (Elacin, ACS) or high-fidelity foam plugs (Etymotic, EarPeace) reduce volume without muddying the sound. Hearing damage from repeated loud exposure is cumulative and permanent. Buy them before you need them.
- **Cash** — most Berlin clubs are cash-only at the bar and at the door. Bring more than you think you'll need; ATMs near clubs charge high fees and often run out.
- **ID** — you need it to get in. A passport or EU ID card is most reliable. Some venues accept driving licences; some don't.
- **A simple dark outfit** — comfortable, breathable, layers. You will sweat. You may be somewhere cold outside and hot inside. Avoid expensive or delicate clothes.
- **Comfortable shoes** — you may be on your feet for 6-8 hours on a concrete floor. Style matters less than your feet.
- **A small amount of food** — eat something substantial before you go. An empty stomach plus a long night is a bad combination.
- **A phone charge** — a small power bank is useful if you're out for a long session.

**Good to have**
- **Lip balm and hand cream** — club environments are dry.
- **Chewing gum** — helps with jaw tension and bad breath from a long night of talking.
- **A reusable water bottle** — if the venue allows it. Some do, some don't. Check.
- **Snacks in your bag** — something for the coat check bag. Nuts, a cereal bar. You may not want to leave to eat for hours.

**Leave at home**
- Anything irreplaceable — phones get lost, bags get broken into coat checks.
- Valuable jewellery.
- A full bag — coat checks charge per item and you don't want to manage a lot.

## Water and Hydration

Drinking enough water is the single most important physical thing you can do at a rave. The combination of dancing, heat, and sustained activity causes significant fluid loss. Dehydration causes headaches, fatigue, muscle cramps, impaired judgement, and in serious cases, dangerous body temperature rises.

How much to drink depends on your level of activity:
- If dancing actively: roughly 500ml (about one large glass) per hour.
- If moving less: 250-300ml per hour is usually enough.
- Don't overdrink either — drinking too much plain water without electrolytes can cause hyponatremia (low sodium). If you're drinking a lot, include an electrolyte drink (sports drink, coconut water) occasionally.

Most Berlin clubs provide free or cheap water at the bar. If a venue doesn't provide water, that's a red flag.

## Food Before and After

**Before:** Eat a proper meal 1-2 hours before going out. Complex carbohydrates and protein (pasta, rice, eggs, meat, legumes) give sustained energy without blood sugar spikes. Avoid a very heavy meal immediately before dancing; avoid going out on an empty stomach.

**After:** After a long night, your body has burned a significant amount of energy and may be running on adrenaline and caffeine. The comedown food that works best tends to be:
- Something warm and easy to digest — soups, eggs, toast, porridge.
- Fruit or juice for quick sugars and vitamins.
- Water, water, water.

Avoid a large processed meal immediately after a heavy night — your digestive system may not cooperate.

## Sleep and Recovery

Sleep is the primary recovery mechanism. After a serious all-nighter:

- Your circadian rhythm is disrupted. It may take 2-3 days to fully normalise.
- The day after a long night, try to sleep at a natural time even if you feel awake on stimulants — the crash will come. Trying to push through another day without sleep makes the recovery worse.
- Light exposure helps reset the clock. Getting daylight (even grey Berlin daylight) the afternoon after a heavy night helps.
- Alcohol is tempting as a sleep aid but degrades sleep quality significantly. If you drink after the night, keep it minimal.
- Magnesium before sleep can help with muscle relaxation and some people find it helps with post-event nervousness.

## The Comedown

After a big night — regardless of what you did or didn't take — many people experience a "comedown" in the 24-72 hours afterwards. This can include:
- Low mood, emotional sensitivity, or mild anxiety (sometimes called "comedown blues" or "Tuesday blues" if the night was a weekend one)
- Physical fatigue and tiredness
- Difficulty concentrating
- Low appetite or cravings for sugar and processed food

This is a normal physiological response. The best mitigation is sleep, food, low-stress activity (walking, gentle exercise), and avoiding alcohol and stimulants during the recovery period.

If the low mood after events is consistently severe or lasts longer than a few days, that's worth talking to a doctor about — it can be a sign that the frequency or intensity of going out is affecting your baseline mental health.

## Harm Reduction

Harm reduction is a public health approach that recognises people make their own choices about drug use and focuses on reducing the risks of those choices rather than pretending they don't exist. Berlin's rave scene has a long-standing harm reduction culture.

**General principles:**
- Know what you're taking. Drug checking services (such as Eve & Rave in Germany) allow you to have substances tested for content and purity before taking them. Using them is not a sign of weakness; it's intelligence.
- Start low. Any new or unfamiliar substance should be taken in a small amount first. Effects vary enormously between individuals and batches.
- Don't mix. Combining substances — including alcohol — multiplies unpredictability and risk.
- Don't go alone. If something goes wrong, you want someone who knows what you've taken and can get help.
- Stay hydrated but not over-hydrated (see above).
- If something feels wrong, stop dancing, find a quiet place, drink water, and ask for help if needed. Most clubs have trained staff.

**Know the symptoms to watch for** (in yourself or others):
- Extreme overheating, not sweating, confusion — possible heat stroke or hyperthermia. Medical emergency: cool the person down and get help immediately.
- Loss of consciousness or unresponsiveness — get medical help immediately.
- Severe anxiety or panic that doesn't reduce — move to a calm space, have someone stay with them, reduce stimulation.

Berlin clubs and major events often have relationships with harm reduction organisations. Eve & Rave Berlin (eveandrave.de) provides drug checking, information, and support at some events.

**Resources**
- [Eve & Rave Berlin](https://eveandrave.de) — harm reduction services, drug checking, support
- [DanceSafe](https://dancesafe.org) — US-based harm reduction organisation with extensive testing information
- Notaufnahme (emergency room): in Germany, dial **112** for ambulance/fire, **110** for police

The goal of all of this is the same as the rest of the rave ethos: arrive informed, take care of yourself and the people around you, and come home.
