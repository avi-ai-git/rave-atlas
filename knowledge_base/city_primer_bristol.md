# City Primer: Bristol, UK

## Scene character
Bristol is the birthplace of the UK bass continuum's most important early chapter. In the late 1980s and early 1990s, the Wild Bunch sound system (which became Massive Attack, Tricky and Portishead's collective milieu) fused hip-hop, reggae, soul, and electronic music into what became known as trip-hop or the Bristol sound. That hybrid, bass-forward, emotionally heavy approach to electronic music is still felt in the city's club culture.

Bristol's current scene is strong for bass music, jungle, drum and bass, dubstep, dub, and leftfield electronic, with a smaller but credible house and techno community. It is a university city with constant fresh energy.

## Key historical context
- Wild Bunch Sound System (1979 onward): the collective that included Massive Attack (3D, Daddy G, Mushroom), Tricky and Neneh Cherry affiliates. Wild Bunch played the Dug Out club and the Thekla boat venue; their eclecticism defined Bristol's sound.
- The Dug Out club (1981 to 1986, Park Row): the spiritual home of the Wild Bunch era. Played soul, hip-hop, reggae, early electro.
- Blue Mountain: a venue in the 1990s and early 2000s associated with drum and bass and jungle nights.

## Key current venues
- Motion: a large warehouse complex (capacity up to around 2,500), Bristol's main club for techno, house, drum and bass, and large-scale bookings. Well-regarded for bookings and sound.
- Lakota: one of the UK's longest-running clubs, in St Pauls (founded 1990s). Three rooms, techno and house focus, marathon parties.
- Thekla: a converted boat on the harbour; live music and club nights, historically significant for Wild Bunch-era events. Indie and eclectic now.
- The Marble Factory: a larger multi-purpose venue for concerts and club nights.
- Strange Brew: a café-bar in Stokes Croft hosting small events, record nights, local DJs.

## Key record shops and labels
- Idle Hands: a record shop in Stokes Croft (opened 2009 by Chris Farrell) and an associated label. Focused on house, garage, dubstep and leftfield electronic. The label has released work from artists including Vessel, Jabu, Kowton, and Peverelist (Tom Ford), and is considered one of the UK's most respected boutique labels.
- Rooted Records: a shop and label associated with roots reggae and dub culture.

## Scene note
Bristol's scene is most distinct from other UK cities in its continuum between reggae/dub sound-system culture and electronic music; that bridge is not just historical but live in current events and labels.

## Suits
- UK bass, jungle and drum and bass fans, dubstep and leftfield heads, anyone who wants the Wild Bunch heritage brought into the present. Less suited for those seeking a pure Berlin-style hard techno experience.
