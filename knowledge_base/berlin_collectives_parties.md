# Berlin Collectives, Itinerant Parties and Underground Series

## Gegen (Radical Queer Techno)

Queer collective, sex-positive party series and techno label launched 2011 by Fabio Boxikus (aka XIK) and Warbear; run today mainly by Fabio/XIK and resident Mar/us. Primarily at KitKatClub roughly every other month (every two months, Friday night to Saturday midday), using the labyrinthine multi-floor layout and pool. Added monthly editions at RSO.BERLIN; collaborates internationally (Athens, Helsinki, Paris, Lyon, UK, Poland). Techno, house, experimental club and pop; residents include La Fraicheur, Mar/us and Mike Starr. Sex-centric, fetish-themed but unpretentious; "wear a shirt and you'll feel overdressed." Tough on overt drug use, strict door to protect a safe queer space. Entry historically around 15 euros. Immersive art, darkrooms and a FLINTA* space at editions like Gegen Aliens.

## Herrensauna (Gay Men's Techno)

Queer hard-techno party that began at Bertrams, the basement floor of Kreuzberg's Loftus Hall. Outgrew it and moved to Tresor, first Sunday nights then Fridays. Raw, hard, fast techno from residents (CEM and others) offset by experimental bookings (Nkisi, Gunnar Haslam, Eomac, Minor Science, Haruka, SPFDJ). Stricter door than typical Tresor; crowd in chains, mesh, fishnets and harnesses, a "trash aesthete" look. Hosted an expansive Tresor31 takeover in 2022 using Tresor, +4 Bar, Globus, Kraftwerk and OHM.

## Pornceptual (Sex-Positive Mixed Queer)

Founded May 2012 by Brazil-born artist Chris Phillips, first as an online queer erotic-art gallery, evolving into a sex-positive dance party. Team includes Emre Busse, Raquel Fedato and resident DJ Diego Garcia (Projekt Gestalten). Over a decade old, one of the city's longest-running sex-positive parties. Held roughly every other month in the labyrinthine rooms of Alte Munze (Molkenmarkt 31; earlier at Prince Charles), with a new theme each edition. Dark, hard, deep pounding techno; leather and latex; said to be less strict in dress than other sex parties. Consensual on-site photography in cordoned-off zones (filming without consent strictly banned). International editions in New York, Paris, Tokyo.

## SchwuZ (Neukolln) -- insolvency 2025, venue closed 1 November 2025

Founded 1977 (from Homosexuelle Aktion Westberlin), Germany's oldest and largest queer club; helped launch Christopher Street Day and the queer magazine Siegessaule. Moved to a roughly 1,600 square metre, around 1,000-capacity Neukolln venue (KINDL site, Rollbergstrasse) in 2013. Per managing director Katja Jager (who took the helm in January 2025), the club ran a monthly deficit of 30,000 to 60,000 euros while revenues declined; it laid off about a third of around 100 staff in May 2025 and filed for insolvency on 1/2 August 2025. An emergency August target of 150,000 euros raised only around 3,000 euros, and a later 300,000 euro anti-investor crowdfunding goal raised only around 52,000 euros. The final Neukolln party was 1 November 2025. The supporting association relaunched parties at varying venues from May 2026 (relaunch at the historic Metropol on Nollendorfplatz). A central case study of Berlin's "Clubsterben."

## Lab.oratory (Berghain Basement, Men's Fetish/Sex Club)

Men-only fetish and sex club in the basement of the Berghain building (Am Wriezener Bahnhof, near Ostbahnhof), with a separate entrance and men-only queue around the corner from the main line. Opened around 2005, founded by Berghain owners Michael Teufele and Norbert Thormann. Each night has a theme and dress code: Thursday typically Naked night (clothes into a bag), Friday "Friday Fuck" 2-for-1 (no specific dress, the most popular general night), other nights speciality fetish (leather, rubber, sports gear, military, masks, fisting, watersports). Doors around 22:00; there is roughly a two-hour entry window with the door locked around midnight, after which you can stay as long as you like. Entry around 10 to 18 euros, cash; a clear bag for clothes and a number written on the shoulder for drink billing. Free condoms and lube, showers. Norms: no phones, photos, videos or naming; no perfume; no chatting or smoking in darkrooms; respect boundaries; "Play Safe." A Lab stamp can give discounted Berghain re-entry but you cannot enter Berghain through Lab.

## Snax and FC Snax (Berghain, Ostgut)

Male-only techno and fetish mega-party run by Ostgut/Berghain, taking over all of Berghain plus Lab.oratory. Began 1992 in a former bunker in Mitte; scaled up at Ostgut (1998 to 2003), moved to Berghain in 2004.

- Snax (Easter edition): the overnight centrepiece of Berlin's annual Easter leather and fetish weekend; dress code "Dirty and Fetish," fetish only.
- FC Snax United (sportswear edition): "Football Club" athletes' party, started 2011, held in November/December (11 November 2023, 3 December 2022 documented); strict dress code sneakers and sportswear, no nudity; men only, "Play Safe, No To Chems."

## Cocktail d'Amore (Historical)

Started September 2009; one of Berlin's top alternative queer parties, hosted by the DJ duo Discodromo (and Berghain resident Boris). Ran the first weekend of the month, day and night, drawing guests from across Europe; Italo disco and house by day building to harder sounds at night; relaxed, sexy, around 85 percent male crowd. Long associated with the Griessmuhle era; a template for sex-positive, music-first queer partying.

## HE.SHE.THEY (Itinerant)

Itinerant global queer-rooted party series, label and fashion brand founded by Steven Braines and Sophia Kearney; idea pitched at Amsterdam Dance Event October 2017, launched 2018/2019 (first party at Ministry of Sound, London). Ethos "a place without prejudice for people to be people," with no dress code, no impossible door, and wall-posted rules banning ableism, ageism, body-shaming, homophobia, misogyny, racism, sexism and transphobia, supported by safety helpers. Aims for 50/50 gender splits and intersectional lineups. Takeovers at Watergate in Berlin and De Marktkantine in Amsterdam, plus London, Paris, Ibiza, New York and LA. House, techno and breakbeat; artists include Honey Dijon, Eris Drew, Octo Octa and Maya Jane Coles.

## GMF Berlin (Sunday Gay Dance Party)

Berlin's long-running Sunday-night gay dance party, over 23 years old; two floors with pop/RnB/hip hop on one and house/electro/techno on the other; younger, relaxed, international crowd. Current venue Ritter Butzke in Kreuzberg, every Sunday (previously House of Weekend at Alexanderplatz).

## Killekill (Label and Party Series, plus Krake Festival)

Founded 2008 by Nico Deuster (DJ Flush, "Bademeister"), first as an events company then a label. Raw, DIY, punk-spirited parties spanning techno, acid, Chicago booty and ghetto house, electro, rave and experimental live electronics with visuals. Began as a weekly Wednesday residency at Berghain Kantine (about two years), then parties at Suicide Circus, Horst Krzbrg and Urban Spree. Sublabels: Vantablack, Killekill House Trax, Boidae, Krake. Killekill co-produces Krake Festival, launched 2010, held annually (historically August, week-long; recent editions in June, for example opening at ://about blank), sharing the anti-headliner, no-advertising ethos; residents include Alienata and Fist Van Odor. Deuster also runs Ick Mach Welle, a mentorship program for disabled electronic musicians.

## Leisure System (Berghain Quarterly Party)

Quarterly party at Berghain and Panorama Bar, an event series turned label, debuted 2008 with Autechre (a turning point for Berghain bookings). Co-founders Ned Beckett and Sam Barker, plus label manager Aaron Gonsher. Leftfield electronic: IDM, electro, bass, genre-crossing; the stated aim is to "inject the theatrics back into a club night." Residents Sam Barker (Voltek), NED, Rob Hall, later Aaron Gonsher. Bookings have included Tessela, Venetian Snares, Andy Stott, Holly Herndon, Special Request, Gesaffelstein, Factory Floor, Kuedo and Disclosure.

## CTM Festival (Club Transmediale)

Annual Berlin festival for adventurous music and art celebrating experimental, contemporary and electronic music. The 27th edition runs 23 January to 1 February 2026 at Radialsystem, Berghain, RSO.Berlin, Volksbuehne am Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz, Morphine Raum and Haus der Visionare, under the theme "dissonate < > resonate," with an opening concert by drone-metal band Earth; co-director Jan Rohlf. The program ranges from doom and drone to experimental rap, hyperpop, noise and bass-heavy Afrofuturist sounds. Held in cooperation with Transmediale. Typically late January and early February.

## Berlin Atonal

Annual experimental festival at Kraftwerk Berlin (relaunched 2013 under Laurens von Oswald and Harry Glass; original run 1982 to 1990 under Dimitri Hegemann, when it incubated Einsturzende Neubauten, Test Dept and Psychic TV). Five days of new commissions, premieres, installations and club nights across Kraftwerk, with late-night programming in Tresor, OHM and Globus. The 2025 edition ran 27 to 31 August. Now an International Biennial Association member. Past collaborators include Actress, Caterina Barbieri, Ryoji Ikeda and Romeo Castellucci.

## Tresor Sub-Series and Klockworks, BPitch, Ostgut Ton

- Tresor sub-series: Klubnacht, New Faces (Wednesdays), Tresor meets / invites, Singularity (Mondays). See Tresor.
- Klockworks: Ben Klock's label, founded 2006; no-nonsense minimal techno (DVS1, Fadi Mohem). Functions more as a label; Klock curates and headlines Berghain and festival showcases.
- BPitch (BPitch Control): Ellen Allien's label, founded 2001. Documented standalone Berlin night: BPitch Control "Pitschnass" at Berghain and Panorama Bar (9 August 2008), curated by Ellen Allien (Modeselektor live, Ben Klock, plus Allien, Chaim, Sascha Funke on Panorama Bar).
- Ostgut Ton: Berghain's label, launched 2005, conceived by Nick Hoppner; first releases by Marcel Dettmann, Cassy, Ben Klock. Closed December 2021 (booking agency 2022). Artists still appear billed under "Ostgut Ton" at Berghain.

## Fusion Festival (Larz, Mecklenburg)

Not Berlin but the spiritual summer pilgrimage of the Berlin scene. Held on a disused Third Reich-era military airfield at Larz (Flugplatz Muritz Airpark), Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, around 160 km from Berlin; began 1997; capacity around 70,000; organised by the non-commercial, non-profit Kulturkosmos association ("holiday communism"). No lineup released beforehand, no commercial sponsors, no VIP, strictly enforced no-photo rule. The 2026 edition runs 24 to 28 June 2026. Tickets are distributed by raffle/lottery: registration opens around early December, with a first raffle mid-December, a payment window through January, and a second raffle in early February for those who missed out. The 2026 ticket is around 220 euros including camping. Tickets are personalised (name and date of birth checked against photo ID at entry); the festival blocks scalped tickets, and a Kulturkosmos Ticket:Bourse allows secure face-value resale.

## Community and Underground Venues

- Stadtbad Oderberger: a restored historic swimming-pool building in Prenzlauer Berg; hosts cultural and occasional electronic events rather than a regular club night (verify recurring series before publishing).
- Silent Green Kulturquartier: a former crematorium in Wedding hosting experimental and electronic events, including CTM programming; not a weekly club.
- Tresor.West: Tresor's Dortmund subsidiary since November 2019; part of the ecosystem, not in Berlin.
