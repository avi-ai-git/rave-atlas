# Sisyphos — Full Profile

## Basic facts

- Full name: Sisyphos (operated by Sisyphos Event GmbH)
- Address: Hauptstrasse 15, 10317 Berlin-Rummelsburg (eastern Friedrichshain, on the Spree)
- Website: sisyphos-berlin.net
- Tickets: shopyphos.com (their own ticketing shop)
- Lost and Found service available via the website

## Location and access

Rummelsburg sits east of Friedrichshain proper, on the bend of the Spree river. The area is semi-industrial, reached most easily by tram (M17 to Kaskelstrasse, then short walk) or by bike along the river. Taxis and Ubers can reach the area but the final stretch is often walk-only. The outdoor area directly borders the Spree.

## Floor layout and spaces

Sisyphos is one of the largest club complexes in Berlin by usable space. The site is a former dog biscuit factory, and the multi-building layout contributes to the festival-like feel.

- Hammahalle: the main room; large industrial hall with full sound system; the peak-time floor for harder, driving techno
- Wintergarten: second main room with a more intimate atmosphere; often used for deeper, more hypnotic programming
- Dampfer (literally "steamer"): the smaller, darker room with an intense concentrated sound; used for the most underground or experimental bookings
- Outdoor area (Garten): large terraced outdoor space with multiple sound areas directly adjacent to the Spree; open when weather allows; the heart of the Sunday afternoon experience

## Opening hours and format

Sisyphos runs a marathon weekend format:
- Doors open Saturday evening, typically around 21:00 or 22:00
- The event runs continuously through Saturday night, all day Sunday, Sunday night, and typically into early Monday morning
- Some editions run to Monday noon or beyond
- The outdoor area opens as the sun rises on Sunday morning; this is the signature Sisyphos moment — 6am-10am in the garden with the Spree view
- Closing time is flexible and weather-dependent; check the website for each edition's expected end

## Lineup and booking

Lineups are almost never announced far in advance. The schedule typically appears a few days before the event via:
- sisyphos-berlin.net (official website)
- sisy.fan (community-built fan tool that aggregates Sisyphos data and unofficial lineup previews)
- Sisyphos Instagram

The booking philosophy mixes known names (Berghain residents, international headliners) with lesser-known DJs in the garden and smaller rooms. Expect a broad spectrum from hypnotic minimal techno to deep house across different floors simultaneously.

## Door policy

Sisyphos has a relaxed but attentive door by Berlin standards. It is notably less strict than Berghain in terms of dress code (no all-black requirement, more mixed aesthetics accepted). Key factors the door considers:
- Sobriety: visibly very intoxicated guests are not admitted
- Groups: large groups of men without women/mixed company get more scrutiny
- Attitude: being calm and present rather than over-eager or performatively cool
- Dress: functional, comfortable club or festival wear; costumes and expressive outfits are common and welcome; extremely formal or overtly tourist-casual dress reads less well

Entry typically runs from around 21:00 to 02:00 Saturday; after that, entry closes until the event's final day phase (Sunday afternoon/evening), when re-entry and fresh entry is sometimes possible.

## Admission and ticketing

Pre-purchased tickets from shopyphos.com or RA reduce door queue time but do not always guarantee entry if the venue is at capacity. For popular editions (holiday weekends, special bookings), tickets sell out. The door price is typically 15-20 euros; special editions can run to 25 euros.

## Character and vibe

Sisyphos has a distinctly different character from the Berghain circuit. It is warmer, more playful, more festival-oriented and less austere. Art installations often appear on-site; the crowd mixes serious Berlin techno heads with a broader international audience including tourists. It is one of the venues that has managed to absorb a larger and more diverse crowd without losing its underground identity.

The Sunday daytime garden experience is iconic: people who have danced all night move outside as the sun rises over the Spree, and the outdoor area becomes its own separate social world running in parallel to the indoor floors. This is the moment many people specifically attend Sisyphos for.

## What to bring and practical notes

- Cash is accepted; ATM on site but can run out on busy nights — bring some cash from outside
- Lockers available for storage
- The Spree is steps away — being near water in early morning is part of the experience
- Dress in layers that can be shed; indoor/outdoor temperature differences are significant
- Cycling to Sisyphos is popular; secure your bike
- The exit/re-entry policy changes by edition; confirm at the door when you arrive

## Sisyphos in the Berlin scene context

Sisyphos sits in an interesting position in the Berlin club hierarchy: it has the scale of a commercial venue but retains enough booking seriousness to be taken seriously by the underground. It is one of the first venues recommended to tourists and newcomers who want the full Berlin experience but may find Berghain's door risk discouraging. It is also where many Berlin locals go for long summer weekends that would be wasted on waiting at Berghain.
