# Electronic Music Genres and BPM Guide

## The Foundation: Four-on-the-Floor and Bar Structure

- Electronic dance music centers on the four-on-the-floor kick drum pattern (kick on every beat: 1, 2, 3, 4)
- BPM (beats per minute) is the genre skeleton - it defines tempo and feel
- Tracks move in phrases of 8, 16, and 32 bars - this is why transitions and builds happen in these intervals
- Off-beat hi-hats and claps add energy and movement to the steady kick pattern
- The bar structure creates predictable build-ups and drops that DJs use for mixing

## The Genealogy: How Electronic Music Evolved

- Disco (1970s) birthed the four-on-the-floor pattern and extended mixes
- Chicago house (early 1980s) emerged when DJs like Frankie Knuckles added drum machines and synthesizers to disco tracks at The Warehouse club
- Detroit techno (mid-1980s) developed when the Belleville Three (Juan Atkins, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson) created futuristic electronic sound influenced by Kraftwerk and the city's industrial decline
- UK explosion (late 1980s) - the Second Summer of Love (1988-89) brought acid house and rave culture to Britain
- European fragmentation (1990s-present) - countless subgenres emerged across Berlin, London, Amsterdam, and beyond

## House Family

### Deep House
- BPM: 120-125
- Feel: Smooth, melodic, soulful with emphasis on basslines and jazz-influenced chords
- Sound markers: Warm pads, jazzy piano, filtered vocals, subtle percussion
- Reference labels: Defected Records

### Tech House
- BPM: 120-130
- Feel: Minimalistic with techy elements, steady grooves, hypnotic repetition
- Sound markers: Stripped-down drums, minimal melodic elements, emphasis on rhythm and groove
- Middle ground between house and techno

### Minimal House / Micro House
- BPM: 115-130
- Feel: Less is more philosophy, emphasis on space and subtle progression
- Sound markers: Funky swung basslines, hard-panned effects, loopy 80s synth leads, clean 707/808/909 drums
- Hypnotic quality through gradual subtle changes

### Progressive House
- BPM: 125-128
- Feel: Layered, evolving tracks with extended build-ups and emotional releases
- Sound markers: Long builds, melodic progressions, anthem-like drops
- Journey-oriented rather than loop-based

### Melodic House & Techno
- BPM: 120-128
- Feel: Emotional melodies combined with driving rhythms
- Sound markers: Lush synth work, emotive chord progressions, deep bass, atmospheric elements
- Bridges the gap between underground and accessible

### Afro House / Organic House
- BPM: 115-125
- Feel: Incorporates African rhythms, percussion, and tribal elements
- Sound markers: Djembe, congas, kalimba, vocal chants, earthy percussion layers

## Techno Family

### Detroit Techno
- BPM: 120-135
- Feel: Futuristic, soulful, influenced by Kraftwerk and funk
- Sound markers: 808/909 drums, warm synth pads, melodic elements, soul influence
- Pioneer artists: Juan Atkins, Derrick May, Kevin Saunderson
- Key track: Derrick May - "Strings of Life"

### Berlin / Industrial Techno
- BPM: 130-140
- Feel: Dark, hard, relentless, physically demanding
- Sound markers: Distorted kicks, metallic percussion, aggressive synths, minimal melody
- The Berghain sound - peak-time intensity

### Hypnotic Techno
- BPM: 130-135
- Feel: Repetitive, trance-inducing, subtle variations create hypnotic effect
- Sound markers: Minimal changes, long loops, emphasis on groove and hypnosis
- Reference labels: Klockworks, Mote-Evolver
- Key artists: Donato Dozzy, Voices From The Lake

### Dub Techno
- BPM: 120-130
- Feel: Deep, atmospheric, influenced by Jamaican dub production techniques
- Sound markers: Heavy reverb, delay effects, deep sub-bass, chord stabs, minimal drums
- Spacious, underwater quality

### Hard Techno
- BPM: 140-150+
- Feel: Fast, aggressive, high-energy, physically intense
- Sound markers: Pounding kicks, industrial sounds, distortion, minimal melody
- Often played in warehouse settings

### Peak-Time Techno
- BPM: 130-140
- Feel: Main room energy, designed for packed dancefloors at 3-5am
- Sound markers: Powerful kicks, driving basslines, big room presence
- The 4am Berghain sound

## UK Bass Continuum

### UK Garage / 2-Step
- BPM: 130-135
- Feel: Skippy, syncopated, vocal-driven, R&B influenced
- Sound markers: Shuffled hi-hats, sub-bass, chopped vocals, swing rhythm
- Laid foundation for dubstep and grime

### Dubstep
- BPM: 138-142 (feels like 70)
- Feel: Heavy sub-bass, sparse, space between beats, wobble bass
- Sound markers: Half-time drums, sub-bass drops, wobble/LFO bass, dark atmosphere
- Emerged from UK garage around 2000

### Drum & Bass / Jungle
- BPM: 160-180 (typically 170-175)
- Feel: Fast breakbeats, heavy bass, high energy, complex drum programming
- Sound markers: Amen break and other chopped breaks, deep sub-bass, rapid hi-hats
- Jungle (1990s): More reggae/ragga influenced, sample-heavy
- Liquid DnB: Melodic, smooth, jazz-influenced (170-175 BPM)
- Neurofunk: Dark, technical, distorted bass (174-180 BPM)
- Jump-Up: Bouncy, energetic, party-focused (172-176 BPM)

### Breakbeat
- BPM: 120-150
- Feel: Syncopated drum patterns, breaks instead of four-on-the-floor
- Sound markers: Sampled breaks (Amen, Apache), varied rhythms
- Foundation of jungle and drum & bass

### Grime
- BPM: 140
- Feel: Aggressive, UK urban, MC-driven, electronic instrumentation
- Sound markers: Sparse, aggressive synths, 140 BPM (doubles as 70), MC vocals

## Trance Family

### Goa Trance
- BPM: 130-145
- Feel: Psychedelic, hypnotic, influenced by Indian spirituality and 1960s psychedelia
- Sound markers: 303 acid lines, layered melodies, ethnic samples, build-and-release structure
- Originated in Goa, India beach parties in the 1980s-90s

### Psytrance (Psychedelic Trance)
- BPM: 140-150 (145 typical)
- Feel: Fast, driving, layered, trippy sound design
- Subgenres: Full-on, forest, dark psy, progressive psy
- Sound markers: Rolling bassline, complex layering, psychedelic effects

### Progressive Trance
- BPM: 128-138
- Feel: Slower than psytrance, melodic, journey-oriented
- Sound markers: Gradual builds, breakdown-focused, atmospheric

### Uplifting Trance
- BPM: 135-140
- Feel: Emotional, euphoric, anthem-driven
- Sound markers: Big melodic leads, emotional breakdowns, hands-in-the-air moments

## Hardcore Continuum

### Gabber / Mainstream Hardcore
- BPM: 150-200+ (nu-style slowed to 160-180)
- Feel: Extremely fast, distorted kicks, aggressive, Dutch origin
- Sound markers: Distorted 909 kick, hoover sounds, aggressive stabs
- Peak era: 1990s Netherlands

### Hardstyle
- BPM: 150-160
- Feel: Hard kicks with reverse bass, melodic elements, euphoric or raw styles
- Sound markers: Pitched kick drums, screeching leads, anthem structure
- Huge in Netherlands and Australia

### Frenchcore
- BPM: 180-230+
- Feel: Faster than gabber, French hardcore variant
- Sound markers: Extremely fast kicks, distorted bass, aggressive energy

### Terror / Terrorcore
- BPM: 200-300+
- Feel: Extreme speed, noise elements, most aggressive hardcore variant
- Sound markers: Impossibly fast kicks, harsh noise, distortion

### Breakcore
- BPM: 160-200+
- Feel: Complex breakbeats, polyrhythms, experimental, genre-blending
- Sound markers: Heavily processed breaks, distortion, delays, samples from any genre
- Experimental and boundary-pushing

## Experimental / Ambient

### Ambient
- BPM: Variable (often no beat or very slow)
- Feel: Atmospheric, textural, designed for listening not dancing
- Sound markers: Pads, drones, field recordings, minimal rhythm
- Created by Brian Eno in 1970s

### IDM (Intelligent Dance Music)
- BPM: Variable
- Feel: Experimental, complex rhythms, home listening rather than club
- Sound markers: Glitchy beats, abstract soundscapes, unconventional structure
- Artists: Aphex Twin, Autechre, Boards of Canada

### Electro
- BPM: 120-130
- Feel: Robotic, funk-influenced, vintage drum machines
- Sound markers: 808 drums, vocoders, funk basslines, retro-futuristic
- Influenced early Detroit techno

### EBM (Electronic Body Music)
- BPM: 120-140
- Feel: Industrial, aggressive, repetitive basslines, influenced punk and new wave
- Sound markers: Sequenced basslines, shouted vocals, industrial sounds

## How to Train Your Ear

### House Recognition
- Four-on-the-floor kick is constant
- Listen for the hat pattern - 16th notes on open/closed hi-hats
- Tempo check: Can you count to 120-130 in a minute while dancing?
- Chord progressions tend to be warm, soulful, jazz-influenced

### Techno Recognition
- Four-on-the-floor but often harder, more driving
- Faster tempo than house (130-145)
- Less melodic emphasis, more focus on rhythm and texture
- Industrial, metallic percussion sounds

### Drum & Bass Recognition
- Unmistakable fast breakbeats (160-180 BPM)
- Amen break is the signature sound (drum pattern from "Amen Brother" by The Winstons)
- Deep sub-bass you feel physically
- Fast hi-hats and snare rolls

### Trance Recognition
- Build-and-release structure with long breakdowns
- Melodic leads and pads dominate
- 130-145 BPM range
- Emotional, euphoric quality with hands-in-the-air moments

### Key Sonic Signatures
- 303 acid squelch: Roland TB-303 bass synthesizer creates signature acid house/techno sound
- Reese bass: Wobbly, complex bassline typical in drum & bass and dubstep
- 909 kick: Roland TR-909 drum machine kick is the techno kick
- Amen break: The most sampled drum break in electronic music (jungle/DnB)
- Hoover sound: Distinctive synth sound in hardcore and gabber
