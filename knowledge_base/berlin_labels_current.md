# Berlin Electronic Music Labels: Classic and Current

## The foundational labels (context, not deep cuts)

### Basic Channel and Chain Reaction
- Mark Ernestus and Moritz von Oswald. Dub techno foundation, 1993 onward. Hard Wax is the associated record shop.
- Chain Reaction (sub-label): Maurizio, Vainqueur, Porter Ricks, Enforcement. Minimal, deep, almost static.

### Tresor Records
- Founded 1991. The Berlin-Detroit connection. First release: X-101 (Underground Resistance). Key back catalogue: Jeff Mills, Robert Hood, Blake Baxter, Juan Atkins, Drexciya, Joey Beltram, Surgeon, Cristian Vogel.
- Current and recent artists: Helena Hauff, DJ Stingray 313, AnD.

### Ostgut Ton
- Berghain in-house label. Founded late 2005, closed December 2021, reopened May 2025. Ben Klock, Marcel Dettmann, Len Faki, Function, Tama Sumo, Prosumer, Norman Nodge. Sub-labels: A-TON and Unterton.

### BPitch Control
- Ellen Allien's label. Founded 1999. Berlin techno and electro. Artists: Ellen Allien, Apparat, AGF, Modeselektor.

### Klockworks
- Ben Klock's label. Hard, hypnotic, industrial Berlin techno. Artists: Ben Klock, Pfirter, Etapp Kyle, Flug.

### Mote-Evolver
- Luke Slater's label. Peak-time and industrial techno, UK-originated but part of the Berlin techno orbit.

## Current labels (the audit gap)

### Leisure System
- A Berlin collective and label for leftfield electronic, ambient and experimental club music.
- Artists include SPFDJ, Rebekah (as collaborator), and experimental acts from the city's underground.
- Party series associated with smaller venues and art spaces.

### Killekill
- Founded by Fantastic Twins (Camille Gillot and Juliane Heinz). Hard EBM, industrial, deconstructed club.
- Artists: Lena Willikens, Petra Flurr, Rar, object blue, Siopis.
- Both a record label and a recurring party series (at about blank, Tresor and others).

### Live From Earth
- Berlin label and collective for house, disco and leftfield electronic. Community-oriented, associated with the city's more melodic underground.

### Giegling
- Technically Weimar-based (Thuringia), with strong Berlin ties. Founded by Leafar Legov (Rafael Leafar), Konstantin, Dwig and Edward.
- Sound: hazy, melodic, warm minimal and micro-house. A direct counter-aesthetic to hard industrial Berlin techno.
- Key artists: Leafar Legov, Konstantin, Dwig, Edward. The label avoids social media and maintains a deliberately obscure presence.

### Dystopian
- Founded by Rebekah (known for powerful peak-time techno) and others. Hard, industrial techno aligned with the Berlin warehouse aesthetic. Artists: Rebekah, Voices From The Lake (Italian, but label-adjacent), Speedy J.

### Uncanny Valley
- A Dresden-based label with significant Berlin scene overlap. Disco, deep house, cosmic. Artists: Young Marco, Move D, Chuckamuck.

### 030 Records (defunct, historic)
- A 1990s Berlin label documenting the early East Berlin rave scene. Historically significant as documentation.

### Innervisions
- Founded 2004 by Dixon (Steffen Berkhahn). Based between Berlin and elsewhere. Deep melodic house and techno; artists: Dixon, Âme (Frank and Kristian from Berlin), Henrik Schwarz, Stimming.

### Afterlife
- Founded 2015 by Tale Of Us (Matteo Milleri and Carmine Cosco, based between Berlin and Milan). Melodic, dark, cinematic techno and house. Artists: Massano, ANNA, Adriatique, Mind Against, Enrico Sangiuliano.
- One of the fastest-growing labels in electronic music in the early 2020s. Polarising: beloved by melodic-techno fans, dismissed by purists.

### Pampa Records
- Founded by DJ Koze. Leftfield, warm, idiosyncratic house and electronic with pop influence. Artists: DJ Koze, Isolee, Lawrence, Robag Wruhme.

### Freude Am Tanzen
- Small label associated with Berlin's minimal era; artists include Tobias Thomas.

## How these labels map to scenes and venues
- Tresor Records and Klockworks and Mote-Evolver: Tresor club main floor, hard industrial Berlin techno.
- Ostgut Ton: Berghain main floor; hard hypnotic techno and Panorama Bar house.
- Basic Channel and Chain Reaction: no dedicated venue; the dub techno sound lives at smaller spaces and on headphones.
- Killekill and Dystopian and Leisure System: about blank, Tresor, the darker/harder end of Berlin's experimental scene.
- Innervisions and Afterlife: less Berlin-club-specific; their sound fits Panorama Bar, Sisyphos, and festival mainstages more than hard-techno venues.
- Giegling: the anti-Berlin techno aesthetic; small stages, intimate rooms, warm and minimal.
- BPitch and Pampa: eclectic, genre-crossing; fit Watergate-era programming, Kater Blau, melodic rooms.
