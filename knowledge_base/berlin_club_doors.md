# Berlin doors and how they think

## Why Berlin doors are strict

- The point of strict doors in Berlin is to curate a crowd that fits the sound, values and intensity of the night, not to create a VIP scene.
- Security watch for obvious drunkenness, big loud tourist groups, people who clearly do not know where they are going and outfits that clash with the club’s culture.
- Calm pairs or trios who know the party or at least a DJ and look like they came to dance blend in much better than large, noisy groups.
- Many clubs enforce camera‑sticker rules so what happens on the floor stays in the room; ignoring that is one of the fastest ways to get rejected or thrown out.

## Common patterns across doors

- **Group size**: 1–3 people is ideal. Four is doable if energy is low‑key and mixed. Big all‑male groups and bachelor parties are red flags almost everywhere.
- **State**: being visibly drunk, on your phone in the queue, shouting in English or treating the line as a photo opportunity all read as “tourist not here for the music”.
- **Intent**: knowing at least one DJ, label or regular party (Klubnacht, Cocktail d’Amore, Herrensauna, Buttons, Pornceptual etc.) signals that you are tuned into the scene.
- **Dress**: comfortable, underground and dance‑able. Dark colours are common but not mandatory; shiny mainstream clubwear, obvious logos and heels you cannot dance in are.
- **Attitude**: doors reward people who can handle a "no" without drama. Arguing, begging or cutting the line will only get you remembered for the wrong reasons.

## Archetypes of door strictness

- **Industrial temples (Berghain, Tresor, RSO)**: the strictest balance of sound, stamina and values. They expect you to be there for the music and the community, not to gawk.
- **Spree playgrounds (Kater Blau, Sisyphos, Wilde Renate)**: still curated but more playful; they are allergic to stag‑party energy and aggressive behaviour, less to colourful chaos.
- **Political / left‑wing hubs (://about blank, parts of RSO)**: doors explicitly screen out racist, sexist, queer‑hostile and macho attitudes.
- **Sex‑positive / fetish clubs (KitKatClub, sex‑positive nights)**: strict dress codes and consent norms; if you are not willing to dress and behave for the context you are not the audience.
- **Tiny underground / afterhours (Golden Gate, Heideglühen)**: doors read more for stamina and respect than fashion, but they still turn away disruptive vibes.
- **Genre‑diverse live venues (Gretchen, Ritter Butzke)**: doors behave more like concert venues – tickets and knowing the show matter most.

## Club‑specific door notes

- **Berghain / Panorama Bar**: mythologised for a reason. Small, calm groups; clothes you can dance in all night; no visible intoxication; know at least the vibe (hard, hypnotic techno downstairs, house / disco / Italo upstairs). Do not rehearse an answer – short and honest is better than a monologue.
- **Tresor**: serious but friendlier to first‑timers; dress simple and club‑appropriate, arrive in small groups and do not treat it as a Berghain consolation prize.
- **RSO.Berlin**: values‑driven and queer‑friendly; treat it like a community space rather than a tourist sight.
- **Kater Blau & Sisyphos**: playful; outfits can be colourful and weird; loud drunk groups in sportswear are still likely to be turned away.
- **KitKatClub**: fetish or costume outfits are mandatory; jeans and a T‑shirt are an automatic "no" even before anyone looks at your face.

## How locals think about "getting in"

- Locals rarely brute‑force the hardest doors week after week; they rotate clubs and party series and accept that some nights start elsewhere.
- People talk about parties (Herrensauna, Cocktail d’Amore, Buttons, Pornceptual, Wax Treatment etc.) at least as much as they talk about venues.
- A "no" at one door often just means "try a different place and come back another weekend" – not a judgement on you as a person.
