# Sven Marquardt and the Berghain Ecosystem

## Sven Marquardt: the doorman

### Who he is
- Born East Berlin, 1964. Grew up in the GDR, where he trained as a photographer and became embedded in the underground punk and subculture scene.
- Heavy facial tattooing, a distinctive presence. His appearance is the most recognised in Berlin club culture.
- He is primarily a fine-art photographer. He works the Berghain door two nights a week (Friday/Saturday Klubnacht) and spends the rest of his time on his photography practice and other projects.
- In 2013 he published his memoir "Die Nacht ist Leben" (English title "The Night Is Life"), which covers his GDR youth, the post-reunification Berlin underground, and his door philosophy. It was translated into multiple languages and was a significant cultural document of Berlin techno.

### His door philosophy (from interviews and memoir)
- "I feel like I have a responsibility to make Berghain a safe place for people who come purely to enjoy the music and celebrate, to preserve it as a place where people can forget about space and time for a little while and enjoy themselves." (GQ interview)
- "It's subjective. Only a few of my guys are allowed to select guests at the door... Beyond that, there are no set rules." (via Mixmag)
- "You always want friction, though. That's the theme in any good club: diversity, friction." (Dancing Astronaut)
- The goal is not exclusion for its own sake but curation: a room where different kinds of people coexist in shared freedom. Marquardt has written that certain markers signal someone is there to gawk or cause trouble rather than be present.
- He describes himself as reading body language, attitude, and energy rather than running a checklist. A decision can be made in under two seconds.
- On the GDR origin: Marquardt has spoken about growing up in a system that monitored and restricted freedom, and how the post-Wall Berlin underground (the techno scene included) was a direct act of reclaiming personal freedom. The door protects that.

### Practical door interaction
- Brief, direct answers are better than elaborate explanations. He is not looking for a story.
- He has described asking questions like "who are you here to see" or "have you been before" not to test knowledge but to get a read on energy and intention.
- Accept rejection without argument. He has stated that arguing never reverses a decision.
- He does not reply to letters or online messages about entry. All requests happen at the door, in person.

## The Berghain ecosystem in depth

### The building
- A former Heizkraftwerk (combined heat and power plant) at Am Wriezener Bahnhof, built 1953 to power the Stalinallee. It supplied the grid until the 1990s.
- Berghain OstGut GmbH purchased the building outright in 2011.
- The turbine hall has ceilings roughly 18 metres high, which gives the sound its characteristic scale.
- The building also houses Halle am Berghain, a large concert and cultural hall used separately for Atonal and other non-club events.

### The floors in detail

#### Berghain (main floor)
- Genre: hard industrial techno, 130 to 138 BPM. Looping, hypnotic, relentless but rarely aggressive.
- Reference residents and bookings: Ben Klock, Marcel Dettmann, Answer Code Request, Len Faki, Function, Blawan, Paula Temple.
- The Funktion-One Dance Stack was installed in 2005 and updated in 2023. One of the loudest and most precise club sound systems in the world. The bass is physical but controlled.
- Lighting: near-total darkness with spare industrial strobes. No lasers, no screens.
- The space is deliberately alienating in scale, making you small and surrendered to the music.

#### Panorama Bar
- Upstairs, smaller, comparatively social. Large windows face east (the sunrise view is legendary).
- Genre: house, disco, Italo-disco, melodic techno, slower BPM range (120 to 128).
- Reference residents and bookings: Tama Sumo, Prosumer, Steffi, Hunee (frequent guest), Honey Dijon, Ryan Elliott.
- The crowd is often described as more mixed and conversational than the main floor; the bar is busier.
- The moment the sun hits the east-facing windows (roughly 6am to 8am on clear mornings) is consistently cited as one of the peak experiences of Berlin clubbing.

#### Saule
- Opened March 2017. Located underneath the main floor, accessed from the entrance level.
- Small, about 200 to 300 capacity, industrial materials.
- Genre: experimental, leftfield, noise, dark ambient, EBM, industrial club music. The most left-field floor.
- Programs separately from Klubnacht; check RA for the Saule lineup.

#### Kantine am Berghain
- A separate building in the Berghain complex, used as a smaller concert, DJ, and cultural event space on non-Klubnacht nights. Friday evenings and specific one-off events. More accessible door than Klubnacht.

#### Halle am Berghain
- A large concert hall in the same complex, used for Berlin Atonal (annual experimental electronic festival) and one-off concerts. Treated as a separate venue from the club.

### The Ostgut Ton label
- Founded late 2005 by Nick Hoppner (then Berghain's booking manager) as the in-house label for Berghain residents.
- Roster: Ben Klock, Marcel Dettmann, Norman Nodge, Steffi, Len Faki, Marcel Fengler, Function, Tama Sumo, Prosumer.
- The label closed in December 2021 and was silent for over three years before reopening in May 2025.
- Sub-labels: A-TON (more experimental, ambient, composed work) and Unterton (live and noise-adjacent).

### Berghain's legal and cultural status
- On 6 September 2016 the Berlin-Brandenburg Finanzgericht (fiscal court, Cottbus, case 5 K 5089/14) ruled that Berghain's Klubnacht sessions qualify as "concerts" under German VAT law, entitling Berghain to the reduced 7 percent cultural rate rather than the 19 percent entertainment rate. The club was represented by Raue LLP. This landmark ruling established that electronic music club nights are culturally equivalent to concerts.
- "Techno Culture in Berlin" was added to the German National Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage on 13 March 2024 as the 150th entry. Campaign led by Rave the Planet gGmbH (co-founded by Dr. Motte, the Love Parade founder, and Ellen Dosch-Roeingh). The original idea to seek UNESCO recognition came from Hans Cousto in 2011.
