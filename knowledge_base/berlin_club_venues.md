# Berlin clubs by sound, rooms and neighbourhood

## Industrial temples

### Berghain, Panorama Bar, Säule and Lab.oratory

- **Location**: Former East German heating plant by Ostbahnhof, on Am Wriezener Bahnhof.
- **Rooms**: Berghain main floor (hard, hypnotic and industrial techno, often 130–138 BPM); Panorama Bar upstairs (house, disco, Italo, more melody); Säule (smaller, darker, often experimental, EBM or leftfield); Lab.oratory in the basement (gay fetish club with its own parties and rules).
- **Sound**: downstairs is defined by residents like Ben Klock, Marcel Dettmann, Norman Nodge and Answer Code Request – long, slowly evolving sets, hard but controlled; Panorama Bar leans to deep, classic and New York house, disco and trippy morning records from residents like Tama Sumo and Prosumer alumni.
- **Events**: Klubnacht is the signature weekender, running from Saturday night into Monday; special events like CTM and label nights use Halle am Berghain, the concert space.
- **Neighbourhood**: slightly detached from the Friedrichshain bar zone but walkable from Warschauer Straße; when you leave on Monday morning you step into a mix of commuters and other ravers.

### Tresor

- **Location**: Köpenicker Straße in Mitte, in a disused heating plant.
- **Rooms**: Tresor basement (raw, industrial techno in a low tunnel with cages and smoke); Globus upstairs (house, dub techno and more groove‑driven sounds).
- **Sound**: legacy tied to Detroit and Berlin – Jeff Mills, Underground Resistance, Juan Atkins – and the Basic Channel / dub‑techno lineage; programming still foregrounds functional, purist techno and deep house.
- **Events**: label nights, album launches and regular weekend parties; entry is typically cheaper than Berghain but still curated.
- **Neighbourhood**: industrial and office buildings along the river; your world is basically the club, the Spree and the S‑Bahn until morning.

### RSO.Berlin

- **Location**: Former Bärenquell brewery in Schöneweide, southeast Berlin.
- **Rooms**: A huge main hall plus smaller floors and a large yard used in summer.
- **Sound**: heavy, rolling techno and tougher house, often with a queer‑underground edge; bookings combine big international names with Berlin crews.
- **Events**: regular weekends and queer party series; consciously positioned as an heir to Griessmuehle’s spirit.
- **Neighbourhood**: post‑industrial riverfront; the trip out feels like leaving the city for a dedicated rave complex.

## Spree‑side playgrounds

### Kater Blau

- **Location**: Holzmarktstraße on the Spree, between Ostbahnhof and Ostkreuz.
- **Layout**: Labyrinth of wooden structures, multiple indoor floors and a boat and riverside terrace outside.
- **Sound**: long, trippy house and techno sets with a strong focus on groove rather than pure hardness; residents carry the old Bar25 / Kater Holzig lineage.
- **Events**: weekender parties from Friday through Monday, plus special daytime events in summer.
- **Neighbourhood**: part of the Holzmarkt collective site, with a village‑like feel and other bars and food options nearby.

### Sisyphos

- **Location**: Semi‑industrial area of Rummelsburg in Lichtenberg, in a former dog‑biscuit factory complex.
- **Layout**: Several indoor floors, a huge garden, food trucks, hammocks and chill‑out areas; wristbands allow leaving and re‑entering.
- **Sound**: across the weekend the sound arcs from housier daytime sets to harder, more hypnotic techno at peak; line‑ups are not heavily advertised but the curation is reliable.
- **Events**: legendary weekender runs where people dip in and out over 48–72 hours; occasional themed parties.
- **Neighbourhood**: feels like travelling to a self‑contained festival zone in the city.

### Wilde Renate

- **Location**: Near Elsenbrücke in Friedrichshain, in connected old apartment buildings around a courtyard.
- **Layout**: Many small, themed rooms with different sonic identities, plus a garden and outdoor bar.
- **Sound**: house and techno with a psychedelic, narrative tilt – more about storytelling and mood than linear peak‑time power.
- **Events**: long‑running party brands and one‑offs; the building layout encourages wandering and getting lost.
- **Neighbourhood**: between Friedrichshain and Treptow, close to other clubs and late‑night food.

## Political and left‑wing hubs

### ://about blank

- **Location**: Ostkreuz, just off Markgrafendamm.
- **Layout**: Multiple indoor rooms and a large tree‑filled garden with its own dancefloor.
- **Sound**: deep house, techno and experimental; line‑ups lean towards crews and collectives aligned with the club’s politics.
- **Events**: many parties explicitly communicate safer‑space and awareness concepts; queer nights and left‑wing fundraisers are part of the programme.
- **Neighbourhood**: dense with other clubs, bars and late‑night food; easy to end up here after house parties in Friedrichshain.

## Sex‑positive and fetish‑focused

### KitKatClub

- **Location**: Near Kurfürstenstraße in Tiergarten.
- **Layout**: Several dancefloors plus play areas, darkrooms and sometimes a pool; lighting puts atmosphere and bodies at centre stage.
- **Sound**: driving techno and house backbone, with the music supporting but not overshadowing the sexual energy of the night.
- **Events**: recurring branded fetish and sex‑positive parties.
- **Neighbourhood**: inner‑city, with a mix of anonymous office buildings and older bars outside; inside is a different universe.

## Tiny underground and afterhours

### Golden Gate

- **Location**: Right under the S‑Bahn tracks at Jannowitzbrücke.
- **Layout**: One cramped main room, low ceiling, simple bar, small outdoor area by the water.
- **Sound**: local minimal, house and techno DJs; sets can feel scrappy or transcendently hypnotic depending on the hour and the crowd.
- **Events**: classic afterhours; opens late and often runs until late morning or into Monday.
- **Neighbourhood**: hard edges of Mitte’s infrastructure; when you step outside it is trains, water and a few kebab spots.

### Heideglühen

- **Location**: At Seestraße in Wedding.
- **Layout**: Open‑air space with wooden huts, covered dancefloors and cosy corners, plus some indoor areas.
- **Sound**: warm, often vinyl‑heavy house and disco with occasional techno; curators favour groove and funk over aggression.
- **Events**: beloved Saturday‑into‑Sunday day and night parties.
- **Neighbourhood**: more residential and local; the club feels like a hidden garden dropped into everyday Berlin.

## Genre‑diverse and live‑leaning

### Gretchen

- **Location**: Kreuzberg, in vaulted former stables.
- **Layout**: Big main hall and secondary spaces suitable for both live shows and club nights.
- **Sound**: drum & bass, dubstep, bass music, IDM, hip‑hop, jazz, global club sounds and more; a go‑to for bass and experimental fans.
- **Events**: concert nights that turn into club nights, label showcases and festival tie‑ins.
- **Neighbourhood**: central Kreuzberg, with bars and late‑night food nearby; easy place to start or end an evening.

### Ritter Butzke

- **Location**: Ritterstraße in Kreuzberg, in an old industrial complex.
- **Layout**: Several indoor floors of different sizes, plus an outdoor courtyard.
- **Sound**: house, techno, tech‑house; bookings blend well‑known names with Berlin residents.
- **Events**: regular Friday and Saturday programming, plus special one‑offs and live‑adjacent shows.
- **Neighbourhood**: lively but not hyper‑touristy Kreuzberg; when you leave there are still people on the streets and food nearby.

## General norms across Berlin clubs

- **Cash**: most serious Berlin clubs are cash-only. Berghain and Tresor are both cash-only; bring at least 50 to 80 euros before arrival. Some newer venues (RSO, OHM) accept card but cash is safer.
- **Cloakroom (Garderobe)**: coat check is mandatory in winter at most venues. Fees are typically 1 to 2 euros, sometimes with a coin-return hook. Large bags are unwelcome and may be refused.
- **No-photo rule**: camera stickers are applied to phone cameras at Berghain, Tresor, RSO, OHM, and about blank. Breaking the rule means immediate ejection and potential ban.
- **SONAR Safer Nightlife**: a harm-reduction and consent awareness initiative active in Berlin's club scene. Club spaces such as Birgit und Bier have hosted SONAR installations providing drug-checking information, anonymous naloxone access, and trained awareness teams on the dancefloor. The programme is part of a broader Berlin model where public health organisations (Fixpunkt, Vista, Schwulenberatung) work directly with club operators.
- **Water**: free or very cheap water fountains are standard at Berghain, about blank, and RSO. Never go more than two hours without hydration.
- **Timing**: Berlin clubs open late and most people arrive between 1am and 3am. Arriving at midnight feels early; arriving at 4am is normal. Afterhours continue until Monday afternoon or later.
