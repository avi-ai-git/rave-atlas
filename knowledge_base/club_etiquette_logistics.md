# Club Etiquette and Practical Logistics

## The Unwritten Rules: Dancefloor Culture

### Give People Space
- The dancefloor is a shared space, not a mosh pit
- If you bump into someone, acknowledge it with a nod or apology
- Do not crowd people, shove, or dance aggressively
- Move around someone, not through them
- If the floor is packed, adjust your dancing to the space available

### Eye Contact and Staring
- Brief eye contact is fine, staring is not
- Do not stare at people dancing
- People come to dance for themselves, not to be watched
- If someone catches you staring and looks uncomfortable, look away

### Phones and Photos
- Keep phone in pocket or bag
- Most serious clubs ban photos (camera stickers placed at door)
- Filming or photographing someone without consent is a violation
- Exception: Sometimes club allows selfies in designated areas, but never film dancefloor
- Be present, do not document

### Consent and Touch
- Never touch someone without clear consent
- Do not invade personal space
- Queer and fetish parties have explicit consent rules posted
- Ask before entering someone's space or starting a conversation
- "No" means no, move on without reaction
- The dancefloor is a safe space for self-expression - protect that

### Energy and Presence
- Bring positive, respectful energy
- Dance for yourself, not to be seen or perform
- If you need to talk, move off the dancefloor
- Respect the music and the moment
- Collective experience - you are part of something bigger than yourself

### Looking Out for Each Other
- If someone seems in distress, check on them or find staff
- Offer water if someone looks overheated
- Help people find friends or exit if they seem lost or confused
- Safe space culture means everyone looks out for everyone
- Better to over-check than ignore someone who needs help

## Door Policy: Understanding and Navigating Selection

### Why Door Policy Exists
- Protecting the vibe, the music, and the culture
- Clubs are private spaces, not public entertainment venues
- Preventing aggressive behavior, gawking, and disrespect
- The door protects the people inside from those who would ruin the experience

### What Bouncers Look For
- **Attitude**: Calm, respectful, not entitled, not visibly drunk
- **Appearance**: Effort without trying too hard, understanding the vibe
- **Group size**: Smaller groups or solo have better odds
- **Behavior in line**: Respectful, quiet, not taking photos
- **Cultural awareness**: Do you understand what this space is?

### What Gets You Rejected
- Large groups (more than 3-4 people)
- Visible intoxication at the door
- Aggressive, entitled, or disrespectful behavior
- Looking like a tourist on a stag do or pub crawl
- Taking photos of the door, building, or bouncer
- Speaking loudly, especially in English in non-English cities
- Asking "why not" or arguing if rejected

### How to Handle Rejection
- Walk away immediately without comment
- Do not argue, ask questions, or demand reasons
- Do not hang around the entrance
- Come back another time or try another venue
- Rejection is not personal - it is about fit and space

## Dress Code: Navigating Club Aesthetics

### Understanding Club-Specific Codes
- Research venue before going - Instagram, Resident Advisor, Reddit
- Berlin aesthetic: All black, minimal branding, casual but intentional
- Fetish nights (KitKat, certain parties): Dress code strictly enforced (latex, leather, lingerie, minimal clothing)
- Beach/summer clubs: More relaxed, colorful acceptable
- Queer parties: Often more creative freedom, but still no tourist outfits

### General Safe Bets
- Dark colors (black, grey, dark blue)
- Minimal branding - no visible logos
- Comfortable shoes (you will dance for hours)
- Layers (clubs are hot, coat check is mandatory)
- Jewelry, piercings, tattoos generally welcomed

### What to Avoid
- Bright colors in serious techno clubs
- Sportswear (tracksuits, football jerseys, basketball shorts)
- Business casual (button-ups, khakis, loafers)
- Designer brands with huge logos
- Costumes, fancy dress, Halloween outfits (unless it is a themed night)
- Anything too new, too clean, or too expensive-looking
- Backpacks (coat check exists for a reason)

## Queue Culture

### In Line Behavior
- Be quiet and respectful
- No loud conversations, laughing, or English in non-English cities
- No phone usage (especially photos)
- Give space to people around you
- Do not cut, push, or try to skip
- If bouncer waves past you, accept it - they are managing flow and mix

### Timing Strategy
- Avoid peak tourist hours (midnight-2am)
- Late arrivals (2-4am) often have easier doors and better music
- Early arrivals (before midnight on Thursdays) can work for some venues
- Monday morning (afterhours) has smallest queues

## Practical Logistics

### Money and Payment
- Many clubs are cash-only (Berghain, for example)
- Bring enough cash for entry, drinks, and coat check (50-100 EUR typical)
- ATMs inside clubs exist but have fees
- Card payments are not universal in European clubs
- Entry fees: 10-30 EUR depending on city and venue
- Drinks: 3-5 EUR beer, 8-12 EUR cocktails (varies by city)

### Coat Check / Garderobe
- Mandatory in most clubs, especially in winter
- Fee: 1-2 EUR typically (often with coin return token system)
- Do not bring large bags - clubs hate this
- Check coat, bag, and anything you do not need on dancefloor
- Keep cash, ID, and keys in pockets

### Hydration and Breaks
- Drink water regularly (most clubs have fountains or affordable water)
- Sip slowly - over-hydration is also dangerous (hyponatremia)
- Take regular breaks in chill-out areas or outside
- Sit down, cool off, rest periodically
- Marathon sessions require pacing

### Transport
- Know how to get home before you go
- Many cities have 24-hour weekend public transport
- Night buses (often prefixed with N) run when trains stop
- Uber/taxis available but expensive
- Sunday morning S-Bahn/U-Bahn often full of ravers going home

### Safety
- Go with people you trust or meet people inside
- Agree to check in with friends periodically
- If someone seems in distress, get help
- Club staff and security are trained in first aid and harm reduction
- Do not leave drinks unattended
- Trust your instincts - if something feels wrong, leave

## How People Actually Find Parties

### Resident Advisor (RA)
- **The** global platform for electronic music events
- Filter by city, date, venue, genre, artist
- Buy tickets through RA platform
- Follow favorite artists and venues for notifications
- Event listings include lineup, times, ticket prices
- RA also has editorial content (interviews, reviews, features)

### Instagram
- Follow venues for lineup announcements (e.g., @berghain, @tresorberlin, @residentadvisor)
- Follow DJs - they post tour dates in stories and posts
- Event announcements often appear in stories before anywhere else
- Use location tags to discover parties

### Word of Mouth
- Ask bartenders, record shop staff, locals
- Reddit city subreddits (r/berlinsocialclub, r/techno, r/aves)
- Some parties are not advertised publicly (invite-only, underground)
- Clubs announce secret parties to mailing lists

### Bandsintown, Songkick
- Follow artists to get notified when they play near you
- Not all electronic music is covered but useful for tracking DJs

### Facebook Events
- Still used for some parties, especially underground ones
- Join local electronic music groups

### Avoid
- Tourist websites with "top 10 clubs to visit"
- Generic pub crawls and club tours
- Anything promising Berghain entry (scams)

## Door vs Presale Tickets

### Door
- Pay cash at entrance
- Risk of being rejected or queue being too long
- Flexibility - decide last minute
- Common for most Berlin clubs

### Presale
- Buy tickets in advance through Resident Advisor, Dice, or venue websites
- Guarantees entry (though still subject to door policy behavior)
- Often cheaper than door price
- Required for major events (Warehouse Project, Printworks, big festival nights)

## Timing: When to Arrive

### Warm-Up (10pm-1am)
- Crowd still arriving, dancefloor not packed
- Resident DJs play, setting the vibe
- Insiders and locals arrive during this window to avoid tourist rush

### Build (1am-3am)
- Energy increases, dancefloor fills
- Transition from warm-up to peak
- Most people arrive during this window

### Peak-Time (3am-6am)
- The core of the night
- Headliners play, dancefloor at capacity
- Hardest music, highest energy
- The reason people go

### Afterhours (6am-12pm+)
- Mellower energy but can still be intense
- Sunrise sets, transitions to daytime
- Crowd thins but diehards remain
- Sunday daytime raving (Berlin specialty)

## Going Solo vs With Friends

### Solo Advantages
- Better door odds (especially Berghain)
- Flexibility to leave when you want
- Easier to meet people and move through crowds
- Focus entirely on music

### Group Dynamics
- Keep groups small (2-3 max for serious clubs)
- Agree on meeting points inside
- Do not cling to each other on dancefloor
- Be prepared to split up and dance separately
- Larger groups often rejected or split at door

## The Chill-Out Room

### What It Is
- Designated rest area away from dancefloor
- Sofas, dim lighting, quieter (though still music playing)
- Safe space to cool down, rest, recover

### When to Use It
- Overheating or exhaustion
- Need to sit and collect yourself
- Overwhelmed by intensity of dancefloor
- Socializing away from loud music

### Rules
- Respect people resting - keep noise down
- Do not stare at people (some may be in distress)
- If someone seems unwell, alert staff

## Club Courtesy

### At the Bar
- Know your order before reaching the bar
- Have cash ready
- Tip bartenders (1-2 EUR or round up)
- Do not wave money or shout
- Be patient during busy times

### In Bathrooms
- Respect privacy
- Do not linger or socialize if line is long
- Clean up after yourself
- Bathrooms are not social spaces during peak hours

### Smoking Areas
- Many clubs have designated smoking areas
- Outdoor areas provide breaks from intensity
- Respect personal space even in smoking area

## What Not to Do

### The Cardinal Sins
- Filming or photographing the dancefloor
- Sexual harassment or non-consensual touching
- Fighting, aggression, or threatening behavior
- Dealing drugs (venues have zero tolerance)
- Destroying property or making a mess
- Being loud, obnoxious, or disruptive
- Treating staff disrespectfully

### Instant Ejection
- Violence or threats
- Non-consensual behavior
- Filming in no-photo venues
- Drug dealing
- Ignoring warnings from staff
